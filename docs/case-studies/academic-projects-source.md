# Academic Case Studies Source Packet

This document is the internal editorial source of truth for the two flagship academic case studies. The original thesis PDFs are private source material and must not be copied into `public/` or linked from the portfolio.

## Shared Editorial Rules

- Present both projects as individual M.Des Interaction Design projects completed at IIT Bombay in 2020.
- Treat all outcomes as exploratory or pilot findings, not commercial impact.
- Preserve participant counts, methods, quotations, timings, and limitations exactly.
- Use "blind users" for participants described as completely blind. Use "visually impaired" only when preserving the original thesis title or discussing broader source material.
- Label present-day interpretation as "Looking back."
- Do not publish student IDs, signatures, approval forms, private recordings, or the thesis PDFs.

## Project II: Teaching Unfamiliar Touch Interfaces to Blind Users

### Source

- Private source: Project II thesis PDF, stored locally and intentionally not committed
- Original title: "Teaching unfamiliar interaction techniques to the visually impaired"
- Context: Individual M.Des Interaction Design Project II, IIT Bombay, 2020
- Guide: Prof. Anirudha Joshi

### Verified Research Record

- Expert discussion: Dr. Charudatta Jadhav
- Field research: five completely blind TalkBack users and instructors at Niwant Andh Mukt Vikasalaya
- Evaluation: six completely blind participants
- Interfaces audited: three time pickers and three date pickers
- Guided-flow average task time: 4 minutes 30 seconds
- Spatial or target-axis average task time: 7 minutes
- Pre-teaching average task time: 12 minutes 30 seconds
- Participant quote: "Oh! It means it's clockwise."
- Participant quote: "Now I want to teach this to my friend in Malayalam."

The reported timings describe a small exploratory pilot. They are not comparative performance claims because the thesis does not provide a controlled baseline or statistical analysis.

### Published Narrative

- Problem: announced controls do not necessarily communicate spatial structure, state, relationships, or gestures.
- Research: expert discussion, interviews, and observation of tactile and structured teaching methods.
- Audit: six picker implementations reduced to seven recurring accessibility problems.
- Concepts: voice, tactile, vibration, spatial audio, hot corners, and guided learning.
- Prototypes: target axis, guided flow, and hot corners.
- Outcome: qualitative pilot findings and exact descriptive task timings.
- Looking back: integrate replayable, progressive guidance into the widget or screen-reader layer and test transfer and retention.

### Limitations

- Small participant group.
- Selected widgets and interaction variants only.
- Medium-fidelity or externally controlled prototypes.
- No longitudinal retention study.
- No production TalkBack integration.

## Project III: Designing a Conversational Railway Enquiry

### Source

- Private source: Project III thesis PDF, stored locally and intentionally not committed
- Product name: Tulasi
- Original title: "A conversational design approach to railway enquiry for Mumbai CSMT"
- Context: Individual M.Des Interaction Design Project III, IIT Bombay, 2020
- Guide: Prof. Ravi Poovaiah

### Verified Research Record

- Station observation: Vijayawada Junction enquiry counter
- Staff research: enquiry-staff interviews at Mumbai CSMT
- Passenger research: three interviews
- Information-architecture study: remote card sort with five users
- Evaluation: seven Android users
- Evaluation instrument: fifteen seven-point Likert questions with mandatory written reasons
- Four of seven participants raised issues with Hindi speech and English or alphanumeric visual information.
- Participant quote: "I find that sometimes it becomes tricky just to use the voice assistant; visual feedback makes it a bit easy."
- Participant quote: "I like the interaction of voice and I think my parents will use this more."
- COVID-19 prevented station deployment of the kiosk concept.

### Published Narrative

- Context: railway enquiry as a fragmented service ecosystem, not a standalone screen.
- Research: station observation, staff interviews, passenger interviews, environment audit, and application review.
- Synthesis: autonomy, active conversation, orientation, governance, and process.
- Concept: self-service kiosk plus cloud service plus conversational agent.
- Architecture: railway information categories translated into intents, entities, states, and visual cards.
- Dialogue: context, implicit and explicit confirmation, disambiguation, progress, and repair.
- Prototype: Twine, Whimsical, Voiceflow, Dialogflow, Google Assistant ASR and TTS, and visual touch interfaces.
- Evaluation: qualitative findings from seven participants without aggregate success claims.
- Looking back: prioritise the service and dialogue model and require live data, language expertise, accessibility, privacy review, and field testing for production.

### Limitations

- Seven-person remote evaluation.
- Three passenger interviews.
- One primary station context.
- Narrow prototype dataset.
- No production railway feed or real payment.
- No station-based kiosk evaluation.

### Prohibited Claims

Do not publish or imply any of the following unless new evidence is supplied:

- A 90% success rate.
- Railway queries completed in under 30 seconds.
- Collaboration with NLP engineers or a production engineering team.
- Commercial deployment, adoption, revenue, or operational impact.
- A production railway feed, payment flow, or station-tested kiosk.
